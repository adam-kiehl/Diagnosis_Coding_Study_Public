# The Impact of AI Assistance on the Reliability and Speed of Veterinary Clinical Diagnosis Coding

This repository contains code for data collection and analysis for a study performed at Colorado State University on the impact of [AI assisted diagnosis coding](https://journals.plos.org/digitalhealth/article?id=10.1371/journal.pdig.0001147) on the 1) reliability and 2) speed of clinical diagnosis coding. The study manuscript can be found on (arXiv)[].

## Abstract

Veterinary medical records represent a large data resource for application to veterinary and One Health clinical research efforts. Use of the data is limited by interoperability challenges including inconsistent data formats and data siloing. Although resource-intensive to perform, clinical coding using standardized medical terminologies enhances the quality of medical records and facilitates their interoperability across sites and species. The study evaluated the ability of a previously developed AI model capable of inferring 7,739 standard diagnosis codes from veterinary free-text to improve the (1) reliability and (2) speed of clinical coding across individuals with varying level of expertise. Relative to manual clinical coding, AI-assisted clinical coding was associated with a model-adjusted 48% increase in inter-coder reliability and a 36% reduction in coding time across professional vetertinary coder, specialty-trained veterinary clinician, and vetarinary student groups. In exploratory group-specific analyses, professional coders were the most comprehensive but showed the smallest reduction in coding time with AI assistance. Veterinary clinicians exhibited relatively low levels of inter-coder agreement and students assigned relatively fewer codes per record. Since clinical coding is resource-intensive and not currently standard in veterinary medicine, AI assistance may provide a practical pathway to unlock significant data to support clinical and translation veterinary research at scale.

## Code Summary

This repository contains the following directories:
- `analysis`: Analysis scripts (`IRR_PowerAnalysis.Rmd` and `Coding Study Analysis.Rmd`) including power analysis, EDA, and results analysis.
- `data_entry`: Streamlit data collection app and associated utilities.
- `fine_tuned`: A placeholder for fine-tuned model state object.
- `plots`: Plots generated by `Coding Study Analysis.Rmd` for the manuscript.

Several files were removed from this repository due to privacy and logistics considerations:
- `analysis/gatortron_predictions.json` contained model predictions for every record.
- `data_entry/records_df.csv` contained clinical text and collected study data.
- `data_entry/CONCEPT_RELATIONSHIP.csv` can be downloaded from [ATHENA](https://athena.ohdsi.org/search-terms/start).
- `fine_tuned/model_save_gator.pkl` can be downloaded from [PhysioNet](https://physionet.org/content/vet-diagnosis-coding/1.0.0/).
