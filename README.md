# The Impact of AI Assistance on the Reliability and Speed of Veterinary Clinical Diagnosis Coding

This repository contains code for data collection and analysis for a study performed at Colorado State University on the impact of [AI assisted diagnosis coding](https://journals.plos.org/digitalhealth/article?id=10.1371/journal.pdig.0001147) on the 1) reliability and 2) speed of clinical diagnosis coding. The study manuscript can be found on (arXiv)[].

## Abstract

Veterinary medical records represent a large data resource for application to veterinary and One Health clinical research efforts. Use of the data is limited by interoperability challenges including inconsistent data formats and data siloing. Clinical coding using standardized medical terminologies enhances the quality of medical records and facilitates their interoperability with veterinary and human health records from other sites. Previous research at Colorado State University developed an AI model capable of inferring diagnosis codes from veterinary clinical free-text by fine-tuning the University of Florida’s GatorTron large language model. The model achieved promising test performance across 7,739 SNOMED diagnosis codes (F1: 76.9, Precision: 81.5, Recall: 74.4). This study expands on these efforts by studying how AI assistance impacts the (1) reliability and (2) speed of clinical coding across a diverse set of clinical professionals. Relative to manual clinical coding, AI-assisted clinical coding was associated with a 48\% increase in inter-coder reliability and a 36\% reduction in coding time across professional coder, clinician, and DVM student groups. Professional coders, while most comprehensive, benefit the least from AI assistance with respect to time. Clinicians exhibited relatively low levels of inter-coder agreement and students assigned relatively fewer codes per record. Both clinicians and students were strongly assisted by AI with respect to time. The results of this study contribute to the body of knowledge surrounding the integration of AI tools into clinical practice and workflows.

## Code Summary

This repository contains the following directories:
- `analysis`: Analysis scripts (`IRR_PowerAnalysis.Rmd` and `Coding Study Analysis.Rmd`) including power analysis, EDA, and results analysis.
- `data_entry`: Streamlit data collection app and associated utilities.
- `fine_tuned`: A placeholder for fine-tuned model state object.
- `plots`: Plots generated by `Coding Study Analysis.Rmd` for the manuscript.

Several files were removed from this repository due to privacy and logistics considerations:
- `analysis/gatortron_predictions.json` contained model predictions for every record.
- `data_entry/records_df.csv` contained clinical text and collected study data.
- `data_entry/CONCEPT_RELATIONSHIP.csv` can be downloaded from [ATHENA](https://athena.ohdsi.org/search-terms/start).
- `fine_tuned/model_save_gator.pkl` can be downloaded from [PhysioNet](https://physionet.org/content/vet-diagnosis-coding/1.0.0/).
